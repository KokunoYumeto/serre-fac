\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:17}
\textbf{17. Extension and restriction of a coherent sheaf.}

Let $Y$ be a closed subspace of the space $X$. If $\mathcal{G}$ is a sheaf on $Y$, we denote by $\mathcal{G}^X$ the sheaf obtained by extending $\mathcal{G}$ by zero outside $Y$; it is a sheaf on $X$ (cf. \hyperref[fac:I:1:no:5]{no.~5}). If $\mathcal{A}$ is a sheaf of rings on $Y$, then $\mathcal{A}^X$ is a sheaf of rings on $X$, and if $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf of $\mathcal{A}$-modules, then $\mathcal{F}^X$ is a sheaf of $\mathcal{A}^X$-modules.

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:17:prop:10}
\textsc{Proposition 10.} \textit{The sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ is of finite type over $\mathcal{A}$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}^X$ is of finite type over $\mathcal{A}^X$.}

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:17:prop:10:proof}
Let $U$ be an open set of $X$, and put $V=U\cap Y$. Every homomorphism $\varphi:\mathcal{A}^p\to\mathcal{F}$ over $V$ defines a homomorphism $\varphi^X:(\mathcal{A}^X)^p\to\mathcal{F}^X$ over $U$, and conversely; $\varphi$ is surjective if and only if $\varphi^X$ is surjective. The proposition follows immediately.

\hyperref[fac:I:2:no:17:prop:10:proof]{The same argument} proves:

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:17:prop:11}
\textsc{Proposition 11.} \textit{The sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ is $\mathcal{A}$-coherent if and only if $\mathcal{F}^X$ is $\mathcal{A}^X$-coherent.}

Taking $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{A}$ gives:

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:17:prop:11:cor}
\textsc{Corollary.} \textit{The sheaf $\mathcal{A}$ is a coherent sheaf of rings if and only if $\mathcal{A}^X$ is a coherent sheaf of rings.}
