\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:13}
\textbf{13. Main properties of coherent sheaves.}

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:13:thm:1}
\textsc{Theorem 1.} \textit{Let}
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow 0
\]
\textit{be an exact sequence of homomorphisms. If two of the three sheaves $\mathcal{F}$, $\mathcal{G}$, and $\mathcal{H}$ are coherent, then so is the third.}

Suppose first that $\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{H}$ are coherent. Locally there is then a surjective homomorphism $\gamma:\mathcal{A}^p\to\mathcal{G}$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the kernel of $\beta\circ\gamma$. Since $\mathcal{H}$ is coherent, $\mathcal{S}$ is of finite type by condition~\hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:def:2:item:b]{(b)}; hence $\gamma(\mathcal{S})$ is of finite type and therefore coherent by \hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:prop:3]{Proposition~3}. Since $\alpha$ is an isomorphism from $\mathcal{F}$ onto $\gamma(\mathcal{S})$, it follows that $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent.

Next suppose that $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ are coherent. Since $\mathcal{G}$ is of finite type, so is $\mathcal{H}$, and it remains to show that $\mathcal{H}$ satisfies condition~\hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:def:2:item:b]{(b)} of \hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:def:2]{Definition~2}. Let $s_1,\ldots,s_p$ be finitely many sections of $\mathcal{H}$ near a point $x\in X$. Since the question is local, we may suppose that there are sections $s'_1,\ldots,s'_p$ of $\mathcal{G}$ such that $s_i=\beta(s'_i)$. Let also $n_1,\ldots,n_q$ be finitely many sections of $\mathcal{F}$ near $x$ which generate $\mathcal{F}_y$ for $y$ sufficiently close to $x$. A family $f_1,\ldots,f_p$ of elements of $\mathcal{A}_y$ belongs to $\mathcal{R}(s_1,\ldots,s_p)_y$ if and only if there exist $g_1,\ldots,g_q\in\mathcal{A}_y$ such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{p}f_i s'_i
=
\sum_{j=1}^{q}g_j\alpha(n_j)
\qquad\text{at }y.
\]
The sheaf of relations among the $s'_i$ and the $\alpha(n_j)$ is of finite type because $\mathcal{G}$ is coherent. Its image under the canonical projection $\mathcal{A}^{p+q}\to\mathcal{A}^p$ is $\mathcal{R}(s_1,\ldots,s_p)$, which is therefore of finite type. This proves that $\mathcal{H}$ is coherent.

Finally, suppose that $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{H}$ are coherent. Since the question is local, we may suppose that $\mathcal{F}$ is generated by finitely many sections $n_1,\ldots,n_q$ and $\mathcal{H}$ by finitely many sections $s_1,\ldots,s_p$; we may also suppose that there are sections $s'_i$ of $\mathcal{G}$ such that $s_i=\beta(s'_i)$. The sections $s'_i$ and $\alpha(n_j)$ then plainly generate $\mathcal{G}$, so $\mathcal{G}$ is of finite type.

Now let $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ be finitely many sections of $\mathcal{G}$ near a point $x$. Since $\mathcal{H}$ is coherent, there are sections $f_j^i$ of $\mathcal{A}^r$ ($1\leq i\leq r$, $1\leq j\leq s$), defined near $x$, which generate the sheaf of relations among the $\beta(t_i)$. Put
\[
u_j=\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_j^i t_i.
\]
Since $\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_j^i\beta(t_i)=0$, the $u_j$ lie in $\alpha(\mathcal{F})$. Because $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent, the sheaf of relations among the $u_j$ is generated near $x$ by finitely many sections, say $g_k^j$ ($1\leq j\leq s$, $1\leq k\leq t$). We claim that the families
\[
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}g_k^j f_j^i\right)_{1\leq i\leq r}
\]
generate $\mathcal{R}(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ near $x$. Indeed, suppose that $\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_i t_i=0$ at $y$, with $f_i\in\mathcal{A}_y$. Then $\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_i\beta(t_i)=0$, so there are $g_j\in\mathcal{A}_y$ such that $f_i=\sum_{j=1}^{s}g_j f_j^i$. Substitution into $\sum_i f_i t_i=0$ gives $\sum_{j=1}^{s}g_j u_j=0$. Thus the family $(g_j)$ is a linear combination of the families $(g_k^j)$, proving the claim. It follows that $\mathcal{G}$ satisfies condition~\hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:def:2:item:b]{(b)}, and the proof is complete.

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:13:thm:1:cor}
\textsc{Corollary.} \textit{The direct sum of a finite family of coherent sheaves is coherent.}

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:13:thm:2}
\textsc{Theorem 2.} \textit{Let $\varphi$ be a homomorphism from a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ to a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{G}$. Then the kernel, cokernel, and image of $\varphi$ are coherent sheaves.}

Since $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent, $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ is of finite type and hence coherent by \hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:prop:3]{Proposition~3}. Applying \hyperref[fac:I:2:no:13:thm:1]{Theorem~1} to the exact sequences
\[
0\longrightarrow\Ker(\varphi)\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\longrightarrow 0
\]
and
\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}
\longrightarrow\Coker(\varphi)\longrightarrow 0
\]
shows that $\Ker(\varphi)$ and $\Coker(\varphi)$ are coherent.

\phantomsection\label{fac:I:2:no:13:thm:2:cor}
\textsc{Corollary.} \textit{Let $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ be two coherent subsheaves of a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{H}$. Then $\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}$ are coherent.}

For $\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G}$ this follows from \hyperref[fac:I:2:no:12:prop:3]{Proposition~3}; as for $\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}$, it is the kernel of the homomorphism $\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{G}$.
